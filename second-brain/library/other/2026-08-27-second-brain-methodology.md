## Summary

- A second brain is only useful if retrieval is easier than re-deriving
  the thought from scratch — otherwise it's just a graveyard of links.
- Two-stage capture (fast inbox note now, full note during a weekly
  review) beats forcing full processing at capture time, because it
  removes the friction that kills most PKM systems within a month.
- Organizing purely by source type (article/book/video) makes storage
  easy but retrieval hard. Organizing by topic (MOCs) makes retrieval
  easy because you browse by the question you actually have later.

## My Take

The failure mode of most "second brain" attempts isn't the tool, it's that
people treat it as archiving instead of thinking. A folder full of saved
articles with no opinion attached is just a bookmarks manager with extra
steps. The valuable unit isn't the source — it's "what I now believe or
would do differently because of this."

## How I'd Apply / Optimize This

- Never let an inbox item sit for more than ~3 weeks unprocessed — treat
  that as a signal to either process it now or admit it wasn't worth
  saving.
- When processing during weekly review, always link the note into a
  topic MOC, even a stub one. An unlinked note is undiscoverable six
  months from now.
- Revisit `topics/*.md` pages periodically — they're the actual index of
  what you think about a subject, and are worth rereading on their own,
  independent of any single source.
